\subsection{Relay Path Analysis (HAPS\texorpdfstring{$\rightarrow$}{->}Gateway\texorpdfstring{$\rightarrow$}{->}UE)}
\label{sec:app-relay}

In addition to the two direct links above (HAPS\,$\rightarrow$\,UE service link,
HAPS\,$\rightarrow$\,Gateway feeder link), Table~\ref{table:app-relay-comparison}
compares the direct HAPS\,$\rightarrow$\,UE link against a decode-and-forward
relay path routed through the Gateway (HAPS\,$\rightarrow$\,Gateway\,$\rightarrow$\,UE).
The Gateway\,$\rightarrow$\,UE access hop reuses the same 2\,GHz band and transmit-power
convention as the direct service link (\texttt{haps\_a2g\_pathloss\_db()}, evaluated
at the Gateway's altitude instead of the HAPS's); the feeder hop reuses the existing
38\,GHz feeder-link model including ITU-P.618 scintillation. The end-to-end relay
capacity is the bottleneck of the two hops, $C_{\text{relay}} = \min(C_{\text{feeder}}, C_{\text{access}})$,
implemented in \texttt{relay\_two\_hop\_capacity\_bps\_hz()}. This is a distinct
concept from the still-unmodeled User\,$\rightarrow$\,UAV\,$\rightarrow$\,HAPS relay
referenced elsewhere in this document (\S\ref{sec:app-arani-table1}).
