## Changes to `HAPS_AI_HW_ChannelModel.tex`

### 1. Section "System Geometry and Link Types" (~line 108-167)

- **Remove** the paragraph at lines 146-148 ("The UAV Relay node's geometry is configured as above, but its SINR performance is not analyzed... left unmodeled.") — no replacement text; the following subsection split makes clear what's modeled.
- **Split** `\subsection{Feeder Link Geometry}` (line 150) into three subsections, in this order: Feeder Link Geometry, Relay Link Geometry, Service Link Geometry.
  - **Feeder Link Geometry**: keep existing content (lines 152-167) essentially unchanged (HAPS-to-Gateway, 38-39.5 GHz, 10-500 m horizontal, near-zenith).
  - **Relay Link Geometry** (new): UAV relay at 500 m AGL (Table 1) to UE, 2 GHz band (same band as service link, but different power/path). Short vertical offset means the link is dominated by horizontal distance and building blockage rather than altitude — attenuation grows sharply once horizontal offset exceeds the LoS-to-NLoS transition set by local building density/built-up ratio (§III.C, Eq. 8-9). This is why, unlike the feeder and service links (whose geometry is fixed by HAPS/Gateway altitude), the UAV relay's placement (altitude + horizontal offset from served users) must be chosen based on local landscape/building statistics. Note 500 m is used as the default placement in this document; per-landscape altitude tuning (trialing multiple altitudes per landscape type and keeping the best) is noted as planned future work, not analyzed here.
  - **Service Link Geometry** (new): HAPS-to-UE, 2 GHz, long range — from 20 km HAPS altitude to UE via slant path, up to the ~100 km service radius (worst-case slant ≈102 km, matching the elevation-angle figures already used in III.B/IV). Contrast with relay: same band as the relay link, but power budget must close over a much longer range with a mostly-unobstructed near-zenith geometry (vs. relay's short-range but building-attenuated path).
  - Add one short linking sentence (either at the end of Feeder or start of Relay) stating relay and service links share the 2 GHz band but differ in transmit power and path characteristics, motivating the split.
